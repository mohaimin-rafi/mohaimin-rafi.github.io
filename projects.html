Undergraduate Thesis

ME400: Project and Thesis (6.0 Credits)

Multiphysics Modeling of Hybrid Laminated Composite Conductors under a DC Field

THESIS SUPERVISOR Dr. Shaikh Reaz Ahmed

Professor, Department of Mechanical Engineering, BUET

TEAM Mohammad Abdul Mohaimin
Md Nahid Shahriare
ABSTRACT

Modern electronics, aerospace systems, and high-power applications increasingly require conductors that combine superior electrical conductivity, high thermal dissipation capability, and structural mechanical integrity. In this undergraduate thesis, a semi-analytical multiphysics modeling framework was developed to predict the coupled electro-thermo-mechanical response of hybrid particle-reinforced laminated composite conductors under steady DC current. A Displacement Function (ψ) formulation was introduced to reduce the coupled 2D mechanical equilibrium equations to a single fourth-order partial differential equation, eliminating the need to solve coupled displacement components simultaneously. Temperature-dependent electrical resistivity and thermal conductivity were incorporated with non-linear Joule heating. The governing equations were discretized using a finite difference network (401 × 101 mesh) and solved iteratively. The model was applied to Al–SiC and Al–Graphene symmetric laminates with continuous power-law volume fraction gradation (Vf = m·xn) and intermediate bonding layers (Silver Filled Epoxy, Graphene-Epoxy, Silver Nanowire Paste). Predictions were validated against Finite Element Method (COMSOL Multiphysics) simulations, demonstrating excellent agreement with over 85% reduction in computational effort compared to fine FEA meshes.

CORE SCIENTIFIC & MATHEMATICAL CONTRIBUTIONS

Formulation of a novel semi-analytical Displacement Function (ψ) reducing two 2D plane elasticity equilibrium equations with thermal gradients to a single 4th-order partial differential equation.

Sequential coupling of electrical field (Ohm's Law with temperature-dependent resistivity ρ(T)), thermal field (heat conduction-convection with non-linear Joule heating G(x,y)), and thermoelastic stress field.

ME 310: Heat Equipment Design Sessional BUET

Design, Fabrication, and CFD Analysis of a Double-Pipe Heat Exchanger with Disc-Type Turbulator and Outside Fins

Designed, fabricated, and computationally analyzed a 1-metre counter-flow double-pipe heat exchanger incorporating a 13-disc perforated turbulator and 8 external longitudinal fins for passive heat-transfer enhancement. Successfully demonstrated a 2 °C temperature drop enhancement directly attributable to the turbulator through comparative 3D CFD simulations.

MY CORE CONTRIBUTIONS
  • Market Sourcing & Fabrication: Procured materials from the local market and led the physical fabrication of the mild-steel heat exchanger prototype in the departmental machine shop.
  • CAD Modeling & Assembly: Executed practical 3D CAD modeling and drafting of the counter-flow assembly, perforated disc turbulator, and external fins.
  • Full 3D CFD Simulation: Conducted comprehensive 3D CFD analysis in SimScale using a 727.7k-cell hexahedral mesh to evaluate flow mixing, recirculation, and thermal performance.
KEY OUTCOMES & PERFORMANCE
  • Thermal Enhancement: Proved an additional 2 °C temperature drop attributable to the disc-type turbulator through comparative simulation runs.
  • Flow Recirculation: Identified vortex breakdown mechanisms and improved boundary-layer interruption behind each turbulator disc.
  • Structural Verification: Verified pressure-boundary structural integrity and leak tightness through hydrostatic pressure testing.

ME 366: Instrumentation & Measurement Lab BUET

Detection of Potential Mosquito Breeding Sites Using UAVs and Deep Learning Computer Vision

Designed, assembled, and field-tested a custom quadcopter UAV integrated with an onboard camera and a deep-learning computer-vision system for detecting potential mosquito breeding sites. While operated manually via controller due to time and budget constraints, the platform successfully achieved high-accuracy aerial detection and geolocation of high-risk stagnant water hotspots.

MY CORE FOCUS: COMPUTER VISION & AI
  • Dataset Preparation & Training: Curated, annotated, and augmented a custom aerial dataset of stagnant water bodies, discarded containers, and rooftop puddles.
  • YOLOv5 Model Implementation: Trained, optimized, and validated a YOLOv5 object-detection architecture specifically tuned for aerial perspectives.
  • Detection Pipeline: Developed the computer-vision processing pipeline to detect and classify potential breeding sites from aerial video feeds in real time.
PROJECT HIGHLIGHTS & FIELD TESTING
  • Flight Platform: Assembled a functional quadcopter UAV with flight controller, brushless DC motors, electronic speed controllers, and remote radio control.
  • Manual Piloting: Conducted targeted aerial surveys via manual controller piloting across difficult-to-reach urban rooftops and open grounds.
  • Site Geolocation: Successfully geolocated and mapped detected breeding hotspots to facilitate targeted civic vector-control interventions.

ME 413: Energy and Environment BUET

Mitigation and Utilization of Methane Emissions from Landfills of Dhaka: Waste-to-Energy Feasibility Study

Conducted a comprehensive environmental and thermo-chemical feasibility study of methane recovery and waste-to-energy conversion from Dhaka's major landfill sites, Matuail and Aminbazar. Modeled methane-generation profiles, compared energy-recovery technologies, and assessed greenhouse-gas mitigation potential through 2045.

METHODOLOGY & ANALYSIS
  • Waste Profiling: Analyzed municipal solid waste intake rates and biochemical composition for Aminbazar (4,220 tons/day) and Matuail (3,256 tons/day).
  • Emissions Modeling: Modeled long-term methane generation trajectories through 2045 using first-order decomposition kinetics calibrated with local waste parameters.
  • Extraction Design: Evaluated engineering strategies for vertical and horizontal landfill-gas extraction wells, headers, and condensate removal systems.
FINDINGS & ENERGY RECOVERY
  • Technology Comparison: Quantified and compared electricity generation potential between anaerobic digestion and incineration pathways based on energy-recovery potential.
  • Pathway Recommendation: Identified anaerobic digestion as the more viable pathway owing to high waste moisture content and low calorific value.
  • Climate Impact: Estimated substantial carbon-equivalent greenhouse gas reduction achievable through active methane capture and power generation.
