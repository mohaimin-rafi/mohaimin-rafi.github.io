Undergraduate Thesis

ME400: Project and Thesis (6.0 Credits)

Multiphysics Modeling of Hybrid Laminated Composite Conductors under a DC Field

THESIS SUPERVISOR Dr. Shaikh Reaz Ahmed

Professor, Department of Mechanical Engineering, BUET

STUDENT AUTHORS (ME BATCH 19, BUET) Mohammad Abdul Mohaimin (Student ID: 1910038)
Md Nahid Shahriare (Student ID: 1910061)
ABSTRACT

Modern electronics, aerospace systems, and high-power applications increasingly require conductors that combine superior electrical conductivity, high thermal dissipation capability, and structural mechanical integrity. In this undergraduate thesis, a semi-analytical multiphysics modeling framework was developed to predict the coupled electro-thermo-mechanical response of hybrid particle-reinforced laminated composite conductors under steady DC current. A Displacement Function (ψ) formulation was introduced to reduce the coupled 2D mechanical equilibrium equations to a single fourth-order partial differential equation, eliminating the need to solve coupled displacement components simultaneously. Temperature-dependent electrical resistivity and thermal conductivity were incorporated with non-linear Joule heating. The governing equations were discretized using a finite difference network (401 × 101 mesh) and solved iteratively. The model was applied to Al–SiC and Al–Graphene symmetric laminates with continuous power-law volume fraction gradation (Vf = m·xn) and intermediate bonding layers. Predictions were validated against Finite Element Method (COMSOL Multiphysics) simulations, demonstrating excellent agreement with over 85% reduction in computational effort compared to fine FEA meshes.

CORE SCIENTIFIC & MATHEMATICAL CONTRIBUTIONS

Formulation of a novel semi-analytical Displacement Function (ψ) reducing two 2D plane elasticity equilibrium equations with thermal gradients to a single 4th-order partial differential equation.

Sequential coupling of electrical field (Ohm's Law with temperature-dependent resistivity ρ(T)), thermal field (heat conduction-convection with non-linear Joule heating G(x,y)), and thermoelastic stress field.

ME 310: Heat Equipment Design Sessional BUET

Design, Fabrication, and CFD Analysis of a Double-Pipe Heat Exchanger with Disc-Type Turbulator and Outside Fins

Team: Mohammad Abdul Mohaimin, Md Nahid Shahriare, Md Shah Wali Ullah  |  Supervisors: Dr. Mohammad Nasim Hasan, Dr. A. K. M. Monjur Murshed

Engineered a double-pipe heat exchanger incorporating internal disc-type turbulators and longitudinal outside fins to enhance thermal mixing and overall heat transfer coefficient. Validated theoretical NTU ratings against 3D hexahedral CFD simulations and conducted full-scale physical workshop fabrication with pressure proof testing.

Objectives & Methodology
  • Thermal rating & analytical modeling using LMTD and ε-NTU methodology in MATLAB.
  • Parametric 3D CFD simulations on SimScale using high-density hexahedral meshing to investigate Dean vortices and turbulent core disruption.
  • Mechanical design, material selection (seamless carbon steel pipe), and shop fabrication drawings per ASME guidelines.
Engineering Outcomes & Results
  • Demonstrated a ~32% enhancement in overall heat transfer coefficient compared to plain concentric pipe configurations.
  • Complete in-house lathe turning, drilling, weld assembly, and piping manifold setup.
  • Successfully passed a 400 psi hydrostatic pressure test with zero leakage or mechanical deformation.
CFD Analysis (SimScale) MATLAB NTU Optimization Fabrication & Machine Shop 400 psi Hydrostatic Test

ME 366: Instrumentation & Measurement Lab BUET

Autonomous Detection of Potential Mosquito Breeding Sites Using UAVs and Computer Vision

Team: Mohammad Abdul Mohaimin, Md Nahid Shahriare, Md Shah Wali Ullah  |  Supervisors: Dr. Md. Ashiqur Rahman, Dr. Kazi Abrar Kabir

Engineered an autonomous aerial surveillance platform integrating custom UAV hardware with an edge-optimized YOLOv5 deep learning object detection model to detect inaccessible stagnant water reservoirs and rooftop mosquito breeding clusters across dense urban areas.

System Architecture & Workflow Overview
1. HARDWARE PLATFORM
  • Carbon Quadcopter Main Frame
  • Multirotor Flight Controller
  • 4× Brushless DC (BLDC) Motors
  • Electronic Speed Controllers (ESCs)
  • FPV Aerial Camera & GPS
2. VISION PIPELINE
  • Autonomous mission path
  • Real-time aerial video capture
  • YOLOv5 model inference
  • Stagnant water identification
  • GPS geotagging & logging
3. CIVIC APPLICATION

Detects hidden mosquito breeding sites on inaccessible sunshades, rooftops, and overhead containers, generating precise GPS logs for targeted public health disinfection.

Objectives & Methodology
  • Assembled a custom quadcopter UAV chassis with ESCs, power distribution board, and telemetry system.
  • Trained a customized YOLOv5 deep learning model on high-resolution overhead imagery of stagnant water bodies.
  • Implemented GPS coordinates logging synchronized with real-time video frames for location tracking.
Engineering Outcomes & Results
  • Achieved high-precision target recognition under varying daytime solar reflection and angle conditions.
  • Successfully demonstrated autonomous aerial surveillance and rapid automated map geotagging of high-risk mosquito breeding hotspots.

ME 413: Energy and Environment BUET

Mitigation and Utilization of Methane Emissions from Landfills of Dhaka: Waste-to-Energy Feasibility Study

Team: Mohammad Abdul Mohaimin, Md Nahid Shahriare, Md Shah Wali Ullah  |  Supervisors: Dr. Mohammad Arif Hasan, Dr. Dipak Kanti Das

Evaluated methane generation potential across major landfill sites in Dhaka using first-order decay kinetic modeling (EPA LandGEM v3.02) and conducted techno-economic feasibility assessment for landfill-gas-to-electricity conversion.

Objectives & Methodology
  • Collected historical municipal waste disposal volume and compositional data for Matuail and Aminbazar dumpsites.
  • Implemented USEPA LandGEM v3.02 kinetic modeling calibrated to humid subtropical climate decay constants.
  • Assessed power generation technologies (internal combustion gas engines, microturbines, flaring systems).
Engineering Outcomes & Results
  • Quantified peak methane emissions and energy recovery potential exceeding multiple megawatts of electrical capacity.
  • Demonstrated substantial greenhouse gas emission reduction and delivered detailed capital cost, payback period, and environmental impact assessment.
EPA LandGEM v3.02 Waste-to-Energy Methane Recovery Anaerobic Digestion
